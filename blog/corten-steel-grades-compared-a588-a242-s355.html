A588 vs A242 vs S355: Corten Steel Grades Compared

Buyer's Guide · 6 min read · Shandong Dazhi Technical Team

"Corten" is a trade name, not a single material. When a buyer asks for Corten steel, the order still has to be written against a real standard — most often ASTM A588, ASTM A242 or EN 10025-5. All three deliver the same basic promise: an alloy steel that forms a dense, adherent rust patina instead of flaking corrosion. But they differ in chemistry, mechanical minimums and where each one is accepted. Choosing the wrong designation is one of the most common — and most expensive — specification errors in weathering steel procurement.

What each standard covers

ASTM A588 is the workhorse structural weathering steel in the US system. It covers Grade A and Grade B plate up to 100 mm (4 in.) for welded bridges, buildings and other structures where weight savings and atmospheric corrosion resistance matter. It was designed to be welded with standard procedures, which is why fabricators default to it.

ASTM A242 Type 1 is the older high-strength low-alloy weathering specification. Its corrosion resistance is at least roughly twice that of carbon structural steel, but it is largely limited to thinner sections — up to about 50 mm (2 in.). Today it is specified mostly in architectural and light structural work: facades, panels, planters and sculpture, where its slightly different chemistry produces an attractive, early patina.

EN 10025-5 is the European structural weathering standard. S355J0WP and S355J2W are the common grades: "S355" gives the 355 MPa minimum yield strength, "W" marks improved atmospheric corrosion resistance, and "P" indicates phosphorus-alloyed grades with higher corrosion resistance for thin, exposed sections. The J0 / J2 suffix sets the impact-test temperature: 0 °C and −20 °C respectively.

Chemistry and mechanical differences

All weathering grades share the same alloying idea: small, controlled additions of copper, chromium and nickel that change how the steel rusts. A242 Type 1 runs a somewhat richer alloy package — notably higher phosphorus — which accelerates and darkens the patina. A588 Gr.A/B keep phosphorus low for weldability and toughness. S355J0WP also allows the phosphorus route; S355J2W does not, and adds the low-temperature impact guarantee.

Mechanically, the grades sit close together. Minimum yield strength decreases with thickness in every standard: A588 Gr.A starts at 345 MPa in thin plate and steps down to 295 MPa in heavy sections; S355 grades hold 355 MPa up to 16 mm and 345 MPa through 40 mm. Tensile ranges overlap in the 435–630 MPa band. In practice the grade choice is driven by the project's design code, not by a large strength gap.

When each grade is specified

  • US structural projects — A588 Gr.A/B is the default wherever AASHTO or ASTM-based design codes govern: bridge girders, transmission towers, freight cars.
  • Architectural work — A242 Type 1 is often chosen for facades, cladding and art installations, particularly in North America, because of its fast-developing, uniform patina in thin gauges.
  • European and international projects — EN 10025-5 grades are required under Eurocode design; S355J2W is the safe choice for outdoor structures in cold climates due to its −20 °C impact rating.

Equivalence notes

Buyers frequently ask whether A588 can replace S355J2W, or vice versa. The honest answer: they are comparable, not interchangeable. A588 Gr.B and S355J2W are close in chemistry and strength, and either would perform similarly in service. But design approvals, impact-testing requirements and welding procedure qualifications are tied to the specified standard. Substituting one for the other requires written approval from the engineer of record — never assume equivalence on paper.

Buyer's tip: When your customer's drawing cites an unfamiliar grade, do not translate it yourself. Send the full standard reference and thickness to the mill and let the grade match be confirmed in writing before production — it costs one email and avoids a rejected shipment.

Which grade should you order?

Follow the design code first: ASTM-based projects point to A588 or A242, Eurocode projects to S355J0WP or S355J2W. Within a system, let thickness and exposure decide. For heavy welded structure, A588 Gr.B or S355J2W. For thin decorative panels where appearance dominates, A242 Type 1 or S355J0WP. If low-temperature service is possible, insist on the impact-tested grade.

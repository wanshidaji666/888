FOB vs CIF vs CFR: Which Trade Term Should Steel Buyers Choose?

The same steel order can cost thousands of dollars more or less depending on the trade term. A buyer's guide with real-world examples.

The 30-second version

FOB: seller delivers on board the ship at the origin port; you arrange and pay ocean freight + insurance. CFR: seller pays freight, you insure. CIF: seller pays freight + minimum insurance.

When FOB makes sense

  • You have a preferred forwarder with better rates (common for regular importers)
  • You want control of the shipping line and transit time
  • Your country imposes special documentation requirements

When CIF/CFR is smarter

  • First-time importers without freight contacts
  • LCL or mixed containers where consolidator relationships matter
  • When the mill's volume contracts give genuinely better ocean rates than you can get

Watch the hidden costs

The quoted freight difference is often smaller than it looks. What actually decides total landed cost:

  1. THC (terminal handling) at both ends — who pays must be stated on the PI
  2. Demurrage — CIF quotes on slow vessels can eat the freight saving if your port is congested
  3. Insurance value — CIF covers only 110% of invoice at minimum cover; high-value cargo may need upgrades

Our recommendation

For first orders, take CIF so the mill is responsible until your port. Once you know your monthly volumes and have a forwarder, switch to FOB and negotiate freight yourself. Ask us for both quotes on the same RFQ — we're happy to show the difference transparently.

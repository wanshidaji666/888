GI Coil Coating Weight Explained: Z60 vs Z120 vs Z275

Choosing the right zinc coating weight decides your product's service life. A plain-language guide for roofing, ducting and appliance buyers.

What the Z-number actually means

Z60, Z120, Z275 are coating weights in grams of zinc per square meter, total both sides (ASTM uses G-values in oz/ft²; JIS uses g/m² per side — always clarify which system a quote uses). More zinc = longer corrosion life, but also higher price and slightly harder welding/forming.

Match the coating to the environment

CoatingTypical environmentCommon products
Z60–Z80Dry indoor, low humidityAppliance back panels, furniture
Z120–Z180General outdoor, urbanRoll-formed profiles, ducting
Z275Coastal, industrial, high humidityRoofing, agricultural buildings

Three questions that prevent disputes

  • Spangle or spangle-free? Spangle-free (minimized spangle) is better when the surface will be painted.
  • Coating tolerance — ask for the actual coating test report per coil, not just the ordered spec.
  • Surface treatment — chromated + oiled is the default for export; confirm if you need RoHS-compliant passivation.

Rule of thumb

Don't over-specify. A Z275 roof in a dry inland climate wastes budget that could go to thicker steel instead. Tell your supplier the application and destination climate — a good mill will recommend the balance point.

ASTM/ASME Standards & Nickel Alloy Plate Tolerances Explained

What SB-127, SB-424, SB-443 and SB-575 actually cover — and why thickness and flatness tolerance classes decide whether your machined part ends up on size.

Specification · 7 min read · ALLOYS Technical Team

Most specification disputes in nickel alloy plate are not about chemistry — they are about what the buyer assumed the standard covered and what the mill actually delivered. This article walks through the four ASME plate specifications we roll against most often, the EN equivalents your European inspection body will ask for, and the tolerance details that matter once the plate reaches the machining shop.

What each specification covers

Each of the four grades we produce has a dedicated ASTM/ASME plate specification. "SB" designations are the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code adoptions of the corresponding ASTM "B" specifications — identical technical content, and the version you cite for pressure-vessel work:

  • SB-127 (ASTM B127) — Nickel-Copper Alloy (Monel 400, UNS N04400) plate, sheet and strip. EN equivalent: 2.4360 (NiCu30Fe).
  • SB-424 (ASTM B424) — Ni-Fe-Cr-Mo-Cu alloy (Incoloy 825, UNS N08825) plate, sheet and strip. EN equivalent: 2.4858 (NiCr21Mo).
  • SB-443 (ASTM B443) — Ni-Cr-Mo-Nb alloy (Alloy 625, UNS N06625) plate, sheet and strip, in Grade 1 (annealed) and Grade 2 (solution annealed) tempers. EN equivalent: 2.4856 (NiCr22Mo9Nb).
  • SB-575 (ASTM B575) — Low-carbon Ni-Mo-Cr alloys (including C-276, UNS N10276) plate, sheet and strip. EN equivalent: 2.4819 (NiMo16Cr15W).

Each specification defines the chemical composition ranges, the minimum mechanical properties, the required heat treatment (temper), permissible manufacturing practice, and the test methods and certification each heat must carry. What they do not fully define on their own is how tightly every dimension must be held — which is where tolerance classes come in.

Tip: Always cite specification plus UNS number. "Monel plate to SB-127" is almost unambiguous, but "C-276 to SB-575" covers several low-carbon Ni-Mo-Cr alloys — UNS N10276 closes the gap. Inspectors reject paperwork for exactly this omission.

Thickness tolerance: the machining-allowance math

For plate, thickness tolerances in the ASTM B463-style framework run from a fixed minus tolerance (typically −0.3 mm) with a plus tolerance that grows with nominal thickness and width. European buyers more often specify EN 10029, which offers four classes:

  • Class A — the default; minus tolerance depends on thickness (e.g. −0.4 mm on 3–5 mm plate, up to −1.0 mm above 25 mm).
  • Class B — fixed −0.3 mm on all thicknesses; the usual choice for machined parts.
  • Class C — zero minus tolerance; all deviation upward. Demanded by flange and component machiners who cannot accept undersize stock.
  • Class D — symmetric ± tolerance where weight-per-piece matters more than floor thickness.

Why this matters: suppose a part finishes at 10.0 mm and your shop needs 0.5 mm of machining allowance per face. Order 11.0 mm plate under a class that allows −0.8 mm, and the delivered 10.2 mm sheet leaves only 0.1 mm per face — barely enough to clean mill scale, let alone flatness deviation. Undersize plate is the single most common cause of scrapped machining lots in nickel alloy, and at 6–8× stainless prices, one scrapped lot erases the savings of a year of careful buying.

Flatness

Flatness deviation for plate is typically limited as a fraction of measured length and width — a common framework allows roughly 0.8% of the smaller dimension for plate under certain thickness ratios, with tighter limits as thickness increases relative to size. Two practical notes:

  • Thin plate (under ~6 mm) in nickel alloys work-hardens during rolling and is more prone to coil memory and waviness than the same gauge in stainless; expect to specify a flatter temper or plan a leveling step.
  • For flame-cut or waterjet blanks, flatness at the machine matters more than flatness on the inspection table — ask us to quote leveled or stress-relieved plate for parts with tight parallelism callouts.

How to order without gaps

A complete nickel alloy plate purchase order states six things, and each omission is an invitation for a surprise:

  1. Specification + UNS number + temper — e.g. "SB-443 Gr. 1, UNS N06625, Grade 1 annealed".
  2. Tolerance class — the thickness standard and class (EN 10029 B or C for machined parts), plus flatness requirements if tighter than default.
  3. Size and quantity — nominal thickness × width × length, per-piece or total weight, and whether random or cut-to-length dimensions are acceptable.
  4. Test requirements — baseline EN 10204 3.1 MTC (always included), plus ASTM G28 intergranular corrosion testing, ASTM A578 ultrasonic examination (Level C for pressure parts) or third-party inspection (SGS / BV / TUV) where the code requires it.
  5. End use and code — pressure vessel, offshore or aerospace duty changes the acceptable tempers and paperwork.
  6. Marking and packing — heat number transfer, branding requirements, interleaving for polished surfaces.

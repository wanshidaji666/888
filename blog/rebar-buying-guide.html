Carbon Steel Rebar Buying Guide: Grades, Standards & How to Avoid Common Pitfalls

HRB400 vs ASTM A615 Gr.60 — what overseas buyers actually need to check before ordering rebar, from yield strength to bundle traceability.

Know your grade naming before you compare prices

Chinese mills quote HRB400 / HRB500 (GB/T 1499), while most international buyers ask for ASTM A615 Gr.60 or BS 4449 B500B. In practice these overlap heavily: HRB400 ≈ Gr.60 ≈ B500B in yield strength (≥400 MPa). What differs is the certification paperwork — make sure the mill test certificate (MTC) states the standard you actually need, not just an "equivalent".

The five things to specify in every RFQ

  • Diameter & length — 6–40 mm; 12 m bars maximize container volume but check your site handling.
  • Standard/grade — name the standard, not a local nickname.
  • Deformation pattern — rib geometry affects concrete bond; ask for the mill's rib schedule.
  • End use — welding on site? Ask for low carbon equivalent (CEV) heats.
  • Inspection — in-house QC report is free; SGS/BV loading inspection is worth it on first orders.

Common pitfalls we see

1. Mixing traded stock with mill material. Some suppliers buy mixed stock to fill small orders — traceability suffers. Insist on heat-number matching between bundles and MTC.

2. Weight-based vs piece-based pricing. Rebar is priced per ton, but shipping is billed by weight or measurement — clarify whether the quote is theoretical or actual weight.

3. Ignoring straightness tolerance. Poorly straightened bars slow down your rebar crew. Ask for straightness ≤ 0.4% of length (straightedge check).

A practical pre-shipment checklist

  1. MTC (EN 10204 3.1) matches heat numbers on bundle tags
  2. Yield/tensile/elongation results for each heat
  3. Bundle count × pieces × unit weight = invoiced weight
  4. Photos of loading, including container seal number
